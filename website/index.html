About this game

MTG Duelstone is a Magic: The Gathering game for your desktop. You sit at a tilted 3D table: your hand at the bottom, your opponents across from you, the stack on the right. Arrows show what a spell targets, and ribbons show who is attacking whom.

The rules come from Forge, an open-source Magic engine that knows about 33,000 cards. It runs priority, the stack, combat, triggers and every card's text, and it plays your opponents. The game never guesses what is legal. It shows you what you can do and asks when there is a choice to make.

Bring your own decks or use the ones that come with it. Paste a list from MTGO or Moxfield, or build one in the deck builder, which suggests cards from thousands of real decks.

  • Every rule and every card's text, from the Forge engine
  • Four formats, four casual variants, cube draft, set draft and sealed
  • Tables of up to four, with friends or the computer in any seat
  • Friends join from a web browser, with nothing to install
  • Deck builder with suggestions, stats and sample hands
  • Your own sleeves, deckboxes and playmats
  • Plays without an internet connection
game modes

Pick how you want to play

Press PLAY and choose a format, then a legality and any variants. The deck list only shows decks that are legal for your choice. Every mode works against the computer and at a table with friends.

Formats

Constructed

60 cards · 20 life

Play anything you like, or pick Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage or Pauper. The game checks your deck against that format's card pool and ban list.

Commander

100 cards · 40 life

One copy of each card, led by a legendary creature. Commander damage is shown next to each life total, and tokens stack up with a count so the table stays readable.

Brawl

60 cards · 25 life

Commander with a smaller deck, built from Standard cards only.

Oathbreaker

60 cards · 20 life

A one-of-each deck led by a planeswalker and its signature spell, both in the command zone.

Casual variants

Planechase

Each game gets a planar deck. Roll the planar die from your command zone, and the plane changes and brings its own rules.

Archenemy

One player gets a scheme deck and 40 life and goes first. Everyone else plays together against them.

Momir Basic

No decklist. Everyone starts with 60 basic lands and the Momir avatar: discard a card to make a random creature of that mana value.

2v2 Teams

Four players in two teams, and a team wins together. Against the computer, the other three seats are AI.

Draft and sealed

Cube draft and set draft

Draft one of the 94 cubes that come with Forge, like the MTGO Vintage and Legacy cubes or a Pauper cube, a real Magic set, or a cube you made. Pods have up to eight seats. Up to three friends can join, and AI drafters take the rest.

Build and play

After three packs, click cards into a 40-card deck and add basic lands, or let the game suggest a deck. Then play one-on-one or at one table for everybody, and go back to the pod for another round.

Sealed

Open six packs of a set, or three to six of a cube, and build from what you get.

Cube designer

Open any cube, edit a copy or start your own. Add cards by search or paste a list from CubeCobra or MTGO.

deck explorer

Every deck in the box

The game comes with thousands of ready-to-play decks: metagame archetypes and real tournament lists for every format, Commander precons, published Brawl and Oathbreaker lists, Forge's quest and theme decks, and starter decks. Browse them by format, look inside any of them, and draw a sample hand.

multiplayer

Play with friends

Tables seat up to four. Host a game, copy the link and send it. Your friends open it in their browser and pick a deck. They don't have to install anything. Seats nobody takes can go to the computer.

Any mode

Commander free-for-all, 2v2 teams, Archenemy, Planechase, or the games after a draft. Everyone uses their own deck.

In the browser

Friends play in Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari. The host's copy of the game serves the browser version.

At home or online

Invite links work on your home network out of the box. To play over the internet, use a VPN like Tailscale or forward a port.

the table

A table you can read

Hover any card for a large preview. Cards you can play, pick or target get a glowing rim. When a spell needs mana, its cost shows as pips and Auto Pay taps your lands for you.

  • Three playmats
  • Hover preview
  • Glow rims
  • x4 stacks
the stack

The stack, and every question

Spells and abilities wait on the stack at the right edge of the table, newest on top. Every choice the rules ask for gets its own dialog: trample damage, trigger order, modes, scry, surveil and values for X.

  • Scry and surveil
  • X values
  • Trigger order
  • Auto-yield
  • Undo
decks

Build and test decks

Search every card by name, rules text, colour, type and mana value. Suggestions come from thousands of real decks, in the style of EDHREC, and work offline. The builder shows your mana curve, colour sources and opening-hand odds, and lets you draw sample hands.

  • Paste-in import
  • Starter decks
  • Forge precons
  • Sample hands
design studio

Sleeves, deckboxes and playmats

Pick a preset or design your own from patterns, colours, emblems, frames and finishes, or print any card's art on them. A live 3D preview shows every change. Use one design for everything or give each deck its own.

Download

Free for Windows, Mac and Linux. Java, the rules engine and the card data come with it, so there is nothing else to install.

Windows

For 64-bit PCs with Windows 10 or 11. Run the installer. It needs no administrator rights and adds MTG Duelstone to the Start menu.

Download for Windows

The installer isn't signed, so Windows may say it protected your PC. Click More info, then Run anyway. When the game first starts, Windows asks whether Java may use the network: allow private networks to host multiplayer games. Playing alone works either way. Rather not install? Get the zip, unzip it anywhere and run mtg-duelstone.exe.

Mac

For Macs with an Apple chip on macOS 12 or later. Open the downloaded file and drag MTG Duelstone into your Applications folder.

Download for Mac

The first time you open it, your Mac says it can't check the app, because the app isn't registered with Apple. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.

Linux

For 64-bit PCs with graphics that support Vulkan (most graphics cards from the last ten years, with current drivers). Download the file, allow it to run and open it.

Download for Linux

To allow it to run, right-click the file, open Properties and turn on Allow executing file as program, or run chmod +x mtg-duelstone-x86_64.AppImage. If it still doesn't open, install libfuse2.

Card images

Card art belongs to Wizards of the Coast, so it isn't in the download. Fetch it once, about 9 GB. On Windows, tick the box at the end of the installer or open MTG Duelstone - Download card images from the Start menu. On Linux, from a terminal:

./mtg-duelstone-x86_64.AppImage --fetch-images

On a Mac: "/Applications/MTG Duelstone.app/Contents/MacOS/mtg-duelstone" --fetch-images. If it stops, run it again and it picks up where it left off.

What's inside

The game, the Forge rules engine and its AI, a Java runtime, Forge's card data and the browser version your friends join with. The engine starts when you open the game and stops when you quit.

Build it yourself

The game is written in Rust with Bevy, the engine in Java. Building needs Rust, JDK 17 and Maven; tools/install-toolchain.sh installs JDK and Maven for your user. The commands are at the bottom of this page.

build from source

Clones the source and Forge, builds the rules engine and the game, and starts it. Building needs Rust, JDK 17 and Maven, the usual Bevy system libraries (ALSA, udev, Vulkan) and about 1 GB of disk for Forge. No JDK or Maven? tools/install-toolchain.sh installs both into ~/.local/opt, no root access needed. tools/fetch-offline.sh downloads the card images.

$ git clone https://github.com/birkmann/mtg-duelstone.git
$ cd mtg-duelstone && tools/setup-forge.sh
$ tools/fetch-offline.sh
$ ./run.sh
